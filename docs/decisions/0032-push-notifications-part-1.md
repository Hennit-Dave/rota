## Problem

An Owner whose renewal failed has 7 days before the team goes read-only (ADR 0031). An email tells them, but a phone buzz is what gets a small-business owner to open the app. The server has to decide who to tell, **when** (the person's own quiet hours, in their own time zone), and **whether** (their per-type switches, their role), send it from a background job (AGENTS.md rule 10: never inside a request), notice phones that no longer exist, and make tapping the notification open the right screen. This part builds all of that and uses it for the two failed-renewal moments. Part 2 adds shift and swap events.

## Options considered

1. **Where the device tokens, deliveries and per-recipient rows live.** (a) On each team's shard, next to the event: a settings change on shard A could not move them in one transaction, which `jobs.md` requires. (b) **All on shard A**, with users, tokens and settings. Chosen: one transaction covers a quiet-hours change and the rescheduling of that person's waiting pushes.
2. **How an event on a team shard becomes rows on shard A.** (a) Write the recipient rows straight from the request or the job after the event commits: a crash in between loses the push. (b) **Two steps.** The event's own transaction (team shard) writes one `push_event` work row; a fan-out job writes one `push` work row per recipient on shard A, unique on (event, user), so a replay does nothing. Chosen: Postgres first, then the queue (AGENTS.md rule 9), and no cross-shard transaction.
3. **FCM or Expo's push service.** (a) Call Google's FCM directly from the worker: a Firebase service-account key on the server, and our own token handling. (b) **Expo's push service**, which calls FCM with the key held at Expo (EAS credentials). Chosen: the worker holds no Firebase or Expo secret. Expo's own page says there is no cost for sending through it (FAQ, read 2026-11-03); its pages do not state FCM's cost (Google's pricing page is the place to confirm; not checked), and Expo states there is no SLA.
4. **An SDK or plain `fetch` for the Expo API.** (a) `expo-server-sdk`: one more dependency. (b) **Plain `fetch`** with a timeout: the two calls we need (send up to 100, get receipts) are small. Chosen.
5. **Device identity.** (a) A separate install id from the app. (b) **The Expo push token is the identity**, unique across the table. Chosen: one fewer thing for the app to store; a token seen under another user moves to them, so a phone handed over stops notifying the first person.
6. **Where the schedule decision lives.** (a) Only at creation. (b) **At creation, again when the job runs, and again when settings change.** Chosen (`jobs.md`): the job reads the recipient's role, toggle, quiet hours and time zone **when it runs**, so a setting changed after the row was made is respected.

## Decision

**Tables** (migration `20261103120000_add_push`, both shards, rows on shard A only; additive). `device_tokens` (user, Expo token unique, a CHECK on the token's shape, `last_seen_at`; deleting a user deletes their tokens). `push_deliveries` (one row per push and device, unique on both; `sending`, `sent`, `failed`; the Expo ticket id; the receipt time; a CHECK that `sent` has a ticket; deleting a token deletes its deliveries). One partial unique index on `work_rows` (`kind = 'push'`, on `payload->>'dedupeKey'`): one push row per event and recipient.

**Routes** (user routes, `security.md` pipeline): `PUT /devices {expoToken}` registers (idempotent; moves a token that was another user's) and `POST /devices/unregister {expoToken}` removes the caller's own (POST because a DELETE body is not reliably carried). Both answer 204, are rate limited per user (`devices_write_user`), and take the user from the session, never from the request.

**Jobs** (`push/jobs.ts`; all are work rows, claimed and recovered like every other kind, and the sweep needs no change because it does not filter by kind):
- `push_event` (team shard): fan-out. Re-checks that the read-only notice is still wanted (the renewal is unpaid), reads each recipient's settings, writes the `push` rows with the first send time already decided.
- `push` (shard A): reads, **when it runs**, the recipient's role in the team (billing goes to the **Owner** of the team and nobody else; an Owner who was replaced or deactivated gets nothing), whether the renewal is still unpaid, their type toggle (**skipped for the two failed-renewal moments**), and their quiet hours and time zone through `decide()`. Inside the window it hands the same row back for the end of it (a new generation, `jobs.md`). Otherwise it writes one `sending` delivery per device **before** the call, makes one call to Expo (batches of up to 100), records each ticket, and deletes a token Expo calls `DeviceNotRegistered`. It then writes one `push_receipts` row for about 15 minutes later.
- `push_receipts` (shard A): asks Expo what happened to each ticket; `DeviceNotRegistered` deletes the token; a receipt Expo does not have yet defers the check from the same row until Expo's own 24-hour clearing (it gives up at 23 hours).

**Quiet hours** (`settings/quietHours.ts`, pure): the window is wall-clock time in the user's time zone, or the team's when they have none (ADR 0024); it may cross midnight; start is inclusive, end exclusive; the delay ends to the second. A `PATCH /settings` of `quietHours` or `timeZone` now runs in one transaction with the rescheduling of that user's `pending` and `claimed` push rows (`push/reschedule.ts`), and the moved rows are enqueued after the commit. An unchanged schedule is left alone.

**Payload:** the phone receives a version, a type, a moment and a team id. No path, no amount, no free text from a user. The title and body are fixed on the event row; they name the team and carry no figure (the amount is on the Billing screen, from the server).

**The app** (JavaScript only; no native change, no dependency change): `registration.ts` (the channel is created **before** the permission is asked for, then the token is fetched and registered; step 0 found that a push naming a channel the phone does not have is dropped silently), `pushTarget.ts` (a known shape opens the billing hub; anything else opens nothing), `usePush.ts` (registers after sign-in; reads the launch response and listens for taps), and a best-effort unregister before the server is asked to revoke the session at sign-out. The team is selected through the same store the team list already corrects if the person is not a member.

**Numbers (approved by Dave, 2026-11-03; chosen, not measured):** `PUSH_SEND_TIMEOUT_MS` 10 000 (the same as the email call); `PUSH_RECEIPT_DELAY_MINUTES` 15 (Expo's own advice: "we recommend checking push receipts 15 minutes after sending"); `RATE_LIMIT_DEVICES_WRITE_USER` 30 per minute (the same as the settings write limit).

## Why

- Re-reading everything when the job runs, and rescheduling when settings change, is what makes quiet hours something a person can trust: the 2am buzz cannot come from a schedule made last week.
- One row per (push, device) written **before** the call is the only way to know, after a crash, that a send may have happened. It is **at-least-once, not exactly-once**: an accepted ticket does not prove the phone has it, and a crash between the call and its record sends again and logs `possible_duplicate`.
- Only a type and ids travel, so a forged or stale notification can point the app somewhere but can never show anything the signed-in person could not already see.

## Consequences and known gaps (written down on purpose)

- **Enhanced push security is not enabled (Dave, 2026-11-03).** Expo's send endpoint "currently does not require any authentication": anyone who learns a device's Expo push token can send that phone a notification, and nothing about the notification would be trusted by the app (it only opens a screen), but it could still show text on the lock screen. The token is never logged, never in a PR or evidence, and never leaves the server. Turning it on later needs an access token created at expo.dev/settings/access-tokens, kept as a server env var, and sent as `Authorization: Bearer`. Revisit before submission.
- **Cold start from a notification needs a preview build to verify.** In a development build the dev launcher opens first: with no app process at tap time, the tap lands on the launcher's own screen and the app's JavaScript never starts, so no app code can act (same as the cold-start link, ADR 0017). After the project is opened from the launcher, the hook reads the launch response and routes correctly (verified). With the app open or in the background it works directly (verified). One earlier cold-start tap, before the hook was instrumented, ended on Schedule; it could not be reproduced in three later attempts. **To verify in the preview build, with the cold-start link test.**
- **A phone that was signed out involuntarily** (the session ended, so there was no access token to call `/devices/unregister`) stays registered until a different person signs in on it (the token moves) or Expo reports it unregistered. Until then it could show that person's notifications on a signed-out phone. Explicit sign-out unregisters first.
- **Daylight saving.** The delay is worked out in wall-clock minutes. Across a clock change inside the window it can end an hour early or late; the job decides again when it wakes, so the worst case is one more short delay, never a push inside a window the person set.
- **No cap on devices per person.** The write limit bounds abuse; a cap is a number for Dave to choose.
- **No TTL is set on a message,** so Expo's default (4 weeks) applies: a phone that comes online weeks later would still show the notice. A failed-renewal notice is worth less each day; choosing a number is Dave's.
- **A push is not a delivery guarantee.** Expo has no SLA; a phone with aggressive battery settings may not receive it with the app closed.
- **The Owner's `notifyBilling` switch** governs only optional billing notices, none of which exist yet. The two failed-renewal pushes ignore it (ADR 0031).
- **Not tested here:** the renewal-paid skip inside the push jobs against a real `renewals` row (the integration tests for the jobs use no renewal id; the check is the same query the email job uses and was exercised by the proofs of ADR 0031). Revisit when part 2 adds a test fixture that makes renewals cheaply.
- Revisit: enhanced push security; a TTL for billing pushes; a device cap; the preview-build cold-start check; part 2 (shift and swap events, which add recipients chosen from the team's members).

## Tests

Unit: quiet hours (22 cases across time zones, half-hour offsets, midnight crossing, the daylight-saving night, the fallback rule), the Expo client's classification and answer checks, the payload and config shapes (`settings/quietHours.unit.test.ts`, `push/expo.unit.test.ts`, `push/message.unit.test.ts`). Integration, on the test shards with a fake Expo (`push/push.integration.test.ts`, 17 tests): devices (move on sign-in, own-token-only removal, the database refuses a non-Expo token), fan-out on shard A and B (one row per recipient, a replay creates nothing, a quiet recipient is scheduled for the end), send (one push per device, Owner only, the toggle skipped for the two moments, quiet hours still apply, settings read when the job runs, no devices, `DeviceNotRegistered`, a retry that sends once, a terminal answer), receipts, and the reschedule from a settings change. The Owner-only test was run with the rule removed and failed, then restored. App: `pushTarget.test.ts` and `registration.test.ts` (order of the steps, nothing logged that could carry a token).
